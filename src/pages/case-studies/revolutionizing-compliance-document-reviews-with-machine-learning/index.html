Overview

A six-week prototype to take the manual work out of compliance review

HatchWorks was engaged by a leading cybersecurity consultancy on a six-week strategic initiative to develop a prototype machine learning service. This service, leveraging the client's data, was designed to predict the compliance status of a document.

Once fully realized, this service promises to significantly reduce the time consultants invest in manually assessing customer compliance documents.

The Challenge

A vast team of consultants, reading documents by hand

The manual review of customer compliance documentation was a significant bottleneck. Employing a vast team of consultants for this seemingly straightforward task was resource-intensive and fraught with inconsistencies and subjective interpretations.

Given their lack of in-house machine learning capabilities, the client sought HatchWorks' expertise.

Where the manual process broke down

  1. 01

    Streamlining workflows for consistency

    Reviews varied from consultant to consultant, with subjective interpretations of the same requirements.

  2. 02

    Upholding quality standards

    Keeping every assessment to the same bar was hard when the work was repetitive and resource-intensive.

  3. 03

    Keeping consultants engaged

    Skilled consultants spent their time on a seemingly straightforward task instead of higher-value work.

The Process

Six weeks to validate feasibility

  1. 01

    Training data from past projects

    The client provided structured datasets from past projects to train the ML model. The data was cleansed and structured for model training.

  2. 02

    PCI-DSS requirements as queries

    Compliance requirements, specifically from the PCI-DSS standard, were ingested and transformed into optimized queries.

  3. 03

    Embedding retrieval, fine-tuned decision

    These queries enabled an embedding model to sift through vast documentation, extracting relevant contexts. Each context was processed by a fine-tuned language model, which determined compliance based on historical data.

  4. 04

    Collaborative validation

    Collaborative virtual sessions with client stakeholders ensured data quality, model accuracy, and alignment throughout.

The Outcome

Feasibility validated, with the benchmark cleared by 30 points

By the project's end, the language model showcased its capability to assess compliance documentation, pinpointing specific text references. Achieving this within six weeks underscored the potential of the approach.

The model reached 80% accuracy against test data, surpassing the initial 50% benchmark set by stakeholders. The prototype validated feasibility and highlighted areas for future improvement, especially with more data.
